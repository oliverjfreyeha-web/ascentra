# Items for counsel review

A running list of questions for a lawyer before launch. Nothing on the site claims attorney approval.

## Images and media
- D4 · The Hall and firefly images (AI-generated with Higgsfield): Higgsfield's commercial-use terms, and any duty to label AI-generated images (see `docs/ASSETS.md`).
- D5 · The four wallpaper images (reef, alpine lake, misty peak, sunrise): their provenance and commercial-use rights. They were provided by the Owner; the original source and license are not confirmed.
- D5 · Whether any of the four wallpapers is AI-generated, and if so how it must be labelled on the site and on `/credits`.

## Safety
- D4 / D5 · Photosensitivity risk for teen users (ages 14 to 17): the moving wallpaper, glints, window lights and fireflies. Current limits: nothing flashes more than 3 times a second, glints repeat no faster than every 6 seconds, and all motion stops with the device's reduced-motion setting or Motion "Off".

## Statements
- D5 · Any user-facing statements about performance or motion, for example "Motion is simplified on this device" in the Appearance panel and the Motion setting descriptions.

## Choose your path (L8)
- L8 · Business topics, side hustles, e-commerce and Amazon topics: confirm they carry no income, earnings or results claims. The site shows "Results vary. Nothing here promises income." on every screen that lists topics, and the topics admin refuses wording like "guaranteed", "passive income" or a dollar amount. Counsel to confirm the wording and whether more disclaimers are needed.
- L8 · Amazon (FBA), Shopify and online marketplaces have their own age rules and adult requirements for seller accounts. Amazon FBA, online reselling and flipping, and dropshipping are hidden from teens (14 to 17). Shopify stores and print on demand are shown to teens: counsel to confirm that is right, or whether they should also be hidden or carry an adult-account note.
- L8 · Affiliate marketing needs disclosure language (for example FTC endorsement rules) wherever it is taught. It is hidden from teens for now; counsel to supply the disclosure wording before a course is published.
- L8 · Teen visibility: five businesses are hidden from teens (online coaching and consulting, Amazon FBA, dropshipping, online reselling and flipping, affiliate marketing). Counsel to confirm this list, and the rule that a teen never sees or picks a hidden topic (the Owner can't assign one either).
- L8 · Whether a Guardian's Privacy Center download of a teen's data should include the teen's five answers and picks. For now it does not (the same rule as the L7 interview).

## Sign-up and sign-in (R1)
- R1 · Second factor is optional for learners and required for Owner, admins and Guardians. Learners (adults and teens 14 to 17) are now allowed in with a password alone; the app recommends an authenticator app on the Account page and once on the learner home, and never requires it. Counsel to confirm this is acceptable, in particular for teen accounts.
- R1 · Sign-up order is now: account → date of birth and US residence → the "Choose your path" questions and picks → plan and 14-day trial. The interview answers are collected before any plan or payment. Counsel to confirm nothing in the Automatic Renewal Terms or the privacy notices needs to change for this order.

## Course structure, videos and the Owner's review (C1)
- C1 · No income claims, enforced automatically. Every course version is checked for text that reads as a promise of income, earnings, profit or results (for example "earn $5,000 a month", "passive income", "guaranteed results", "quit your job", "six figures"), in lesson text, practice items, video briefs and transcripts. A lesson with any of these can't go to review, a module with any can't be approved, and the check shows the exact words and where they are. A price for a service ("charge $500 a month for the retainer") is not treated as a claim. Counsel to confirm the list of patterns (`lib/courses/income.ts`) and whether anything else should be blocked.
- C1 · "Reviewed by the Owner on {date}" appears under a lesson only when the database holds the Owner's recorded approval of that exact version (insert-only `module_reviews`, also in the audit log). Otherwise the lesson says "Reviewed by an ASCENTRA reviewer" (a Reviewer verified it) or nothing. Counsel to confirm the wording, and that neither label implies legal, professional or attorney review.
- C1 · Course videos are made and uploaded by the Owner; the app never generates video. Each video needs a transcript or captions before the Owner can approve it, and learners can read the transcript instead of watching. Counsel to confirm whether a full transcript is enough for accessibility law (for example ADA or WCAG captions), or whether timed captions are needed before launch.
- C1 · Practice spaces ("sandboxes") are simulated and labelled "Practice (simulated)": made-up data only, never a live service, account or real person's data. Counsel to confirm the label is clear enough, including for teens.
- C1 · Videos are stored privately and learners get links that work for ten minutes, only for a course they may open (teen-hidden and unpublished rules apply). Counsel to confirm nothing else is needed for teen learners watching videos that may show the Owner's face or voice.

## Progress, side hustles, missions and the leaderboard (C2)
- C2 · Teen real-world missions. Some practice items ask the learner to do something in the real world (post publicly, observe a business with no contact, create a listing, contact a business by email or form, interview a possible customer). For a teen (14 to 17) a mission opens only when all of these hold: their state is on the Owner's allow-list for that kind of mission (off in every state until the Owner turns it on); their verified Guardian approved real-world missions for that course (once per course; the Guardian sees the request in the Guardian Center, not by email); and, for a mission that contacts someone, the Guardian approved that one mission this time (each approval is used once). These limits always apply to teens: no messaging private individuals, no meeting anyone in person, no sharing personal details, no handling real client money or accounts. Adults need no Guardian step. Counsel to decide, per state and per kind of mission, where teens may do them (child labor, online safety and privacy rules), and to confirm the limits and the Guardian wording.
- C2 · The learner's US state is asked at sign-up (after the date of birth, optional there, editable in Account) and stored privately (learner, server and authorized staff only; the audit log records that it was set, never the state). Counsel to confirm the notice text and whether the privacy notices need to mention it.
- C2 · Leaderboard. Adults (18+) appear only after choosing a nickname (filtered; never their real name or email) and can opt out in Account; it shows nickname, rank and streak only, with no chat, stickers, profile links or messages. The Owner can remove a nickname, with a reason, audited. Teens are never shown to others: their board shows simulated "practice rivals", each labelled "Practice rival (simulated)", plus their own row. No Guardian approval is asked for the teen practice board. Counsel to confirm the adult default (on once a nickname is chosen), the nickname rules, and that simulated rivals shown to teens are acceptable and clearly enough labelled.
- C2 · Community links (the Owner's Discord and social links) are on a Community page, labelled as outside links that open in a new tab; shown to teens unless the Owner hides them (default: shown). Counsel to confirm whether teens should see them by default, and each linked service's own age rules (Discord and most social platforms set a minimum age of 13, some higher by region).
- C2 · Cost-to-start and market-outlook estimates on business and side-hustle cards. Every number is entered by the Owner with sources and a "checked on" date, and the card says "Estimate, not a promise. Checked {date}. Sources."; with no data it says "Estimate coming". The income-claims check runs on every text field. Counsel to confirm the wording and whether more disclaimers are needed (FTC rules on earnings claims and business opportunities).
- C2 · Course notices (up to two per course, shown once to each learner): a business license notice and a software or AI plan notice. General information only, never sponsored, no legal advice, no income claims; refused if they contain sponsor or promo-code wording. Counsel to confirm the standard wording, and that these don't amount to legal advice.
- C2 · Capstones in business courses include "Automation with AI": what is automated, master prompts, and up to three AI plans (each with a price, a source link and a checked-on date), entered by the Owner only. With no plans listed the page says "A paid plan may be needed" and recommends nothing. Counsel to confirm that naming third-party products and prices is fine without an affiliation disclosure (there is none), and the "Not sponsored" note.
- C2 · Licenses and terms for links and open-source resources used in courses and capstones (third-party sites, tools, templates, code). Counsel to confirm a check of each resource's license and terms of use before a course is published, and whether attributions are needed.
- C2 · Optional AI summaries of a learner's Notebook (off by default), including for teens. Only the course's own auto-notes are sent (never the learner's private "My ideas" journal, never a name or email), through the existing AI provider and spend caps, at most 3 a day per learner, and logged in ai_calls. Counsel to confirm whether teens may turn this on themselves or whether a Guardian setting is needed, and whether the privacy notices need to mention it.
- C2 · Twelve topics moved from "business" to "side hustle" (same content, same teen settings; existing picks follow). Counsel to confirm the L8 teen rules still read correctly with side hustles (for example, affiliate marketing, Amazon FBA, dropshipping and online reselling stay hidden from teens).

## Course import (I1)
- I1 · Imported course content is supplied by the Owner (written with the Owner and pasted or uploaded as one file). It is never published by the import: every imported course starts as a Draft and goes through the usual review, a Reviewer's verification of each lesson, and the Owner's approval of each module before it is published. Counsel to confirm this review flow is enough for Owner-supplied content.
- I1 · Resource links in an imported course need a license and terms check. A resource whose terms the Owner hasn't checked (`termsChecked: false`) is stored but never shown to learners. Counsel to confirm what a "terms checked" resource needs before it is shown, and whether attributions are needed.
- I1 · Content drawn from videos and articles must be summarized in ASCENTRA's own words and credited, never copied. The import keeps each source's credit line and license class (`web_summarize_only` sources may only be summarized). Counsel to confirm the summarizing and crediting rules, including for YouTube videos.
