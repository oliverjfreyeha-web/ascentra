How you can actually invest

Open to anyone: equity crowdfunding (Reg CF) on Wefunder, StartEngine, Republic or DealMaker. Anyone 18+ can invest, often from $100. If you are not accredited, yearly limits apply based on your income and net worth, and the platform checks them for you. Shares are hard to sell for years.

Private rounds: most startups here raised from venture funds. Getting in usually means being an accredited investor (about $200k income, $300k with a spouse, or $1M net worth not counting your home) and knowing the founders or joining an angel syndicate.

How these numbers were made

  • Scores are estimates, rounded to 1 to 10, drawn from public industry reports and market knowledge as of October 2026. They are a starting point for research, not verified data.
  • Competitor counts are order-of-magnitude estimates. Sources disagree a lot: for example, worldwide SaaS company counts range from about 30,000 to 80,000 (Ascendix, BloggersPassion).
  • Region views apply each region's share of companies and local adjustments (VC depth, local giants, cost of building) to the global estimate.
  • Opportunity score weights giant control most, then crowding and growth, then margins, scalability, funding, failure risk, speed and startup cost.
  • Named startups come from 2026 funding news and deal trackers (Qubit Capital, CB Insights, Dealroom, raising.fi, Axios Pro, PitchBook, funding.tech.eu and others). Each row links its source. Round sizes were not checked against company announcements, and euro and pound rounds are converted to dollars approximately. Rounds of $20M or more are tagged "Seed (large)" because they are no longer small.
  • Source tags on named startups: high means a news outlet or the company's own announcement, medium means a deal database, low means a list site or a single promotional page.
  • Not financial advice. Early-stage startups fail more often than not. Only invest money you can afford to lose.
